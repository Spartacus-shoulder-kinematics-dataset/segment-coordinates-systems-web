Rotation sequences

A joint orientation is one rotation matrix R = RparentT Rchild, but it can be described by twelve different intrinsic sequences of three angles. Choose a sequence and angles, look at the intermediate frames (′, ″) and the rotation axes, then click another sequence to see the same orientation re-expressed.

Reading the table

  • Cardan (Tait–Bryan) sequences use three different axes. The middle angle is limited to ±90°, and gimbal lock happens at ±90°. The ISB lower-limb, spine and shoulder girdle JCS are Cardan sequences (ZXY, YXZ).
  • Euler (proper) sequences repeat the first axis. The middle angle is in 0–180° (or its negative twin), and gimbal lock happens at 0° and 180°. The ISB humerus uses YXY: at 0° elevation the plane of elevation and axial rotation merge.
  • The first rotation is about an axis of the parent (e1), the last about an axis of the child (e3) and the middle one about the floating axis e2. This is the Grood & Suntay JCS read as an intrinsic sequence.
